Council approves plan to reopen the Millrace footbridge

The 1931 truss bridge over the Millrace Canal has been fenced off since 2023. Repairs could start in April, with the crossing open again by fall.

By Dana Whitfield, Courier staff

Published Nov. 5, 2026, 9:30 a.m. Updated 4:10 p.m.

Email

The Harlow Valley City Council voted 6–1 Wednesday night to spend $2.4 million repairing the Millrace footbridge, the narrow steel crossing that connected the east side to downtown for more than 90 years before inspectors closed it.

The bridge was fenced off in March 2023 after a state inspection found corrosion in two of its floor beams. Since then, people walking from the Eastgate neighborhood to Canal Street have had to detour roughly half a mile along Route 9, which has no sidewalk for part of the way.

“We heard from parents, from people who work downtown, from folks who don’t drive,” said council member Andre Fields, who sponsored the measure. “This is the cheapest way we have to make that walk safe again.”

Marisol Ortega, who has run a bakery on Canal Street for 22 years, watched the vote from the second row. Este puente es parte de nuestra historia, she said afterward — this bridge is part of our history. “Half my morning customers used to walk over it.”

The lone vote against the plan came from council member Ruth Ellison, who said she supported reopening the bridge but wanted the city to seek more state money first. “I don’t want local taxpayers carrying a bill the state has helped cover in other towns,” she said.

What happens next

The city will put the work out to bid in December. County engineer Paul Mercado said repairs would replace the corroded beams, add a new deck and bring the railings up to current height standards while keeping the bridge’s original trusses.

If bids come in on budget, construction could begin in April and the bridge could reopen by October 2027, Mercado said. The city has applied for a state grant that would cover up to half the cost.

Residents can review the engineering report and the project timeline on the city’s project page. A public meeting on the design is scheduled for Jan. 14 at the Harlow Valley Public Library.

Editor’s note

This story was updated at 4:10 p.m. to add comments from county engineer Paul Mercado. An earlier version misstated the month the bridge closed. It closed in March 2023.
